## 4.12 Chapter Summary

The dual-tier system was evaluated against 5 hypotheses across 3 specified experiments, plus a supplementary cross-submission matching investigation undertaken because Experiment 3 raised a question the original design left open, an empirical AI-authorship characterisation, and an external benchmark comparison against published literature spanning retrieval algorithms, AI detection, and LMS integration architectures. Of the 5 hypotheses, one is clearly supported (F1), one is clearly not supported (Tier 1 resolution rate), one is supported only at the component level and not at the true end-to-end level (latency), one is supported in substance despite a strict literal target not being met (real-time feasibility), and one requires reporting its 2 halves separately because they diverge (cost-effectiveness).

Four major findings recur across this chapter and are treated as interconnected rather than coincidental. First, the escalation rate this corpus genuinely requires under this system's precision constraint (approximately 47%) is the single root cause behind both Hypothesis 1's shortfall and the cost half of Hypothesis 2's shortfall. This is not treated as an isolated or unmotivated result: external literature on adaptive query routing (Arabzadeh et al., 2021) independently found that a selective-escalation strategy operating on roughly half a workload achieves higher recall than naive allocation of the same budget (0.96 versus 0.91 at a 50% budget), supporting the general principle that gated escalation on this scale is a productive, not merely tolerable, operating point for a system bridging sparse and dense retrieval — without that paper predicting or establishing 45-50% as a universal equilibrium value this system's own result happens to confirm. Second, 10 real implementation defects were found and corrected specifically because results were checked against ground truth at every stage rather than trusted once produced, and in 3 cases a first, plausible-looking fix was itself wrong and only caught by the same discipline. Third, the AI-authorship detection evaluation revealed high precision (88.2%) but very low recall (16.7%) that collapses on newer models (GPT-4: 6.9%), aligning directly with independent multi-tool benchmarks (Weber-Wulff et al., 2023) and theoretical detection bounds (Sadasivan et al., 2024), thereby providing empirical justification for the system's advisory-only governance model. Fourth, benchmarking the system's integration against LMS architectural studies (Foltýnek et al., 2020; Reducindo et al., 2017) provides general external context for the value of a self-hosted, purpose-built integration over commercial alternatives, consistent with this thesis's own cost and workflow results; the specific architectural decision to decouple compute-intensive detection into an asynchronous Celery/Redis service layer, operate on-premise for FERPA/GDPR compliance, and render highlighted passage evidence directly into Moodle rests on this thesis's own load-testing and design evidence (§3.4.4, §4.9.2-4.9.3), not on an external citation for that specific mechanism.

Chapter 5 discusses these findings, architectural implications, and future research directions.
